01 / Legends of the hills

Every flame carries a story.

Passed down through generations, these two beloved traditional accounts explain why the hills light their lamps again after Diwali.

FOLKLORE / I

The homecoming of Madho Singh Bhandari

In a cherished Garhwali telling, the commander Madho Singh Bhandari served King Mahipat Shah and led a military expedition toward the Tibetan frontier, often associated in local accounts with Dapaghati. With the warriors still away at Diwali, villages waited anxiously. When the soldiers were said to have returned victorious eleven days later, the hills erupted in celebration — a second festival of lights born from relief and reunion.

A legend of valour and homecoming
FOLKLORE / II

When joyful news crossed the mountains

Another traditional explanation says word of Lord Rama's return to Ayodhya travelled slowly to remote Himalayan settlements. Eleven days after the plains celebrated, the news finally reached these highland communities. Families lit lamps and rejoiced in their own mountain Diwali, an enduring symbol of distance bridged by shared joy.

A legend of light reaching every home

These are oral traditions, not conclusively established historical explanations. Stories, spellings, and customs differ across Uttarakhand's valleys.

02 / Rituals & traditions

Rooted in the land.
Alive in the people.

Celebrated through fire, gratitude and dance, Igas is as much about village community as it is about festival lights.

🔥

Bhelo Khelna

भैलो खेलना

Bundles of dry, locally available wood — including Bhimal in some traditions and resinous pine in others — are tied, lit, and whirled in radiant circles, often to the beat of dhol-damau. A striking expression of the festival's spirit.

🌾

Pashu Dhan Puja

पशुधन पूजा

Cattle and oxen, vital to traditional mountain agriculture, are washed, honored and fed special offerings, including grain-based pinda in some communities. An act of gratitude for the animals that sustain rural life.

🥁

Chhura & Jhumelo

छुड़ा और झुमेलो

Neighbors gather in village courtyards (kholi) for communal songs, dancing and the resonant rhythms of dhol-damau. Folk performances and their names vary between villages.

03 / The heart of Igas

The whirling Bhelo.

A living circle of fire against a Himalayan night. The bhailo (also spelled bhelo) is more than a torch — it represents celebration, movement, shared courage and belonging.

Traditionally, villagers gather outdoors, light specially prepared wooden bundles and swing them in glowing arcs while others sing and play dhol-damau. Materials and methods vary by locality.
